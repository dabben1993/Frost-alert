# Digest: WhatsApp Business/Cloud API (round 1)

## Findings

1. **[mechanism]** Cloud API supports interactive reply-button messages (`type: interactive`, `button` action); tapping fires a `messages` webhook with `interactive.button_reply.id`/`title` — a self-hosted endpoint can parse this for acknowledgment. — Meta for Developers docs, confidence: high. https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/messages/interactive-reply-buttons-messages/
2. **[mechanism]** Onboarding path for a single developer: Meta Developer account → app with WhatsApp Business Messaging → WABA → Meta auto-generates a free test number → permanent System User token. No pre-existing registered company strictly required at this stage. — Meta docs, confidence: high.
3. **[mechanism]** The Cloud API number cannot be the same number active on consumer WhatsApp Messenger (must be separate or removed from consumer app first); must receive SMS/voice OTP; Meta explicitly lists "Personal number" as "Not Recommended." — Meta docs, confidence: high.
4. **[mechanism]** Free auto-generated test number limited to 5 OTP-verified recipients and widely reported (n8n community Dec 2025-Jan 2026; 2026 Medium guide) to expire/get deleted roughly every 90 days, requiring manual recreation and webhook re-subscription — real recurring maintenance burden. — n8n community / Medium, confidence: medium.
5. **[mechanism]** New Business Portfolios start at TIER_250 (250 unique business-initiated recipients/24h) without formal Business Verification — ample for a single-recipient bot; reaching 2,000+ requires full verification. — Meta docs, confidence: high.
6. **[mechanism]** Free-form ("session") messages only work inside a 24h window opened by the recipient's last inbound message; any business-initiated cold-start message (i.e. the "move your bonsai" alert) must use a pre-approved template — gated behind template approval even for a single self-message. — Meta docs (updated 2026-08-05), confidence: high.
7. **[pricing]** Conversation-based billing replaced by per-delivered-template-message billing effective 2025-07-01. Non-template messages inside an open window are free (since 2024-11-01); utility templates inside an open window have been free since 2025-07-01, but Meta's own roadmap states that from **2026-10-01** utility messages within the service window also become chargeable, ending that exemption. — Meta docs (updated 2026-08-05), confidence: high.
8. **[pricing]** From 2026-10-01, Meta introduces a free monthly allowance of 1,000 service (non-template, in-window) messages per business phone number; only the 1,001st+ is billed — does NOT cover the cold-start template message that opens each escalation. — Meta docs, confidence: high.
9. **[pricing]** Sweden is grouped in Meta's "Rest of Western Europe" pricing region (with Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, Ireland, Norway, Portugal, Switzerland) — stated directly in Meta's pricing docs. — Meta docs, confidence: high.
10. **[pricing]** For "Rest of Western Europe," third-party BSP rate-card republications put per-delivered utility-template rate at ~$0.018-0.020 USD (two independent BSP sources: HighLevel $0.0180, SleekFlow/Manychat $0.0197) — could not render Meta's own interactive rate-card tool directly, so corroborated by resellers rather than Meta's raw table. — HighLevel/SleekFlow, confidence: medium.
11. **[pricing]** At this rate, a hobby bot sending a few dozen escalation alerts/season to one Swedish recipient would cost on the order of single-digit-cents to ~$1/year — not literally $0, but negligible. — inference from #10, confidence: medium.
12. **[policy]** WhatsApp Business Terms of Service (last updated 2024-02-16) state the signer "will use our Business Services solely for business, commercial, and authorized purposes, and not for personal use," and separately prohibit use "for personal, family, or household purposes" under Acceptable Use — **directly conflicts** with a single-hobbyist personal-use deployment. — WhatsApp/Meta official ToS, confidence: high. https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/business-terms/
13. **[policy]** Breach gives Meta the right to "limit, throttle, suspend, or terminate" the account "within our reasonable discretion" — enforcement against a near-zero-volume personal bot is undocumented/discretionary, not automatic or codified. — same source, confidence: high.
14. **[policy]** For EEA users the contracting entity is WhatsApp Ireland Limited, but terms still permit data transfer/processing "to the United States and other countries globally" regardless of where the service is used — relevant to a Sweden-based user's data-residency expectations. — same source, confidence: high.
15. **[policy]** No 2025-2026-dated Meta announcement found specifically tightening restrictions on hobby/personal/low-volume use beyond the long-standing (≥Feb 2024) "not for personal use" clause — absence-of-evidence, not confirmation of non-enforcement. — confidence: medium.
16. **[reliability]** Marketing/aggregator claims of "98-99.5%" global and "99-99.5%" Europe delivery rates are unsourced within the article and written in promotional register — red flag per source-craft rules, low confidence, not a verified benchmark. — SMSGatewayCenter vendor blog (2025), confidence: low.
17. **[reliability]** An engineering-oriented vendor blog argues WhatsApp can "fail silently" — a message can sit "sent-but-not-delivered" for hours with no clear failure signal if the recipient is offline, unlike SMS's carrier-level delivery-report codes — implying it's not ideal when a hard timeout/fallback signal is needed. Vendor blog, not empirically benchmarked. — SMSGatewayCenter, confidence: low.

## Leads (not chased)
- Meta's own interactive rate-card tool renders via client-side JS and couldn't be captured as static text — worth fetching with a JS-capable tool for a first-party Sweden/Rest-of-Western-Europe number.
- No timed benchmark for template-review turnaround/rejection rate for a simple single-button "utility" template in 2025-2026.
- No enforcement case studies found for near-zero-volume personal-use bots — only anecdotal test-number-expiry threads.
- "Coexistence" mode (linking an existing consumer number without deleting it) mentioned once on Reddit but not verified in Meta's own docs.
- No Sweden-specific individual (non-corporate) first-hand account of completing Meta Business Verification.

## Not found
- No primary-source (Meta) numeric confirmation of the exact Sweden/Rest-of-Western-Europe rate — relied on two converging third-party BSP citations.
- No dated 2025-2026 Meta policy change narrowing hobbyist access beyond the pre-existing ToS clause.
- No rigorous non-vendor benchmark of WhatsApp push-delivery reliability to stock Android.
- No Sweden-specific business-verification acceptance/rejection data for individuals.

## Verdict
Technically capable but not a clean fit. Mechanically strong: reply-button messages + webhook payloads are a documented, primary-source-confirmed two-way-ack mechanism (high confidence); onboarding doesn't require a registered company or full Business Verification at this volume (Tier 250 default is ample, high confidence); marginal Meta cost for Sweden-region alerts is roughly $0.02/message or a few dollars/year at most (medium confidence), not literally $0. However, the decision's core premise — personal use by a single hobbyist — sits in direct, currently-live tension with WhatsApp's own Business ToS, which explicitly prohibits "personal, family, or household" use (high confidence, primary source); this is a pre-existing rule, not a new 2025-2026 crackdown, and enforcement against near-zero-volume hobby bots is undocumented/discretionary. Combined with real onboarding friction (dedicated phone number, template approval gating even the cold-start message, free test numbers expiring ~every 90 days per community reports, medium confidence) and unverified/aggregator-only reliability claims, WhatsApp Cloud API is workable but requires knowingly operating outside the letter of Meta's ToS plus modest recurring maintenance — weaker fit than channels with no such tension.
