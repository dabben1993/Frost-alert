### Leads

- Verify directly on AWS's categorized Free Tier catalog page (aws.amazon.com/free/serverless or aws.amazon.com/free/) whether EventBridge Scheduler's 14M-invocation allowance is formally labeled "Always Free" (perpetual) the same way Lambda's is — the pricing page text describes the allowance but I did not see the explicit "Always Free" badge/label applied to Scheduler specifically in what was fetched.
- For long-term (multi-year) hobby use on AWS, confirm whether choosing the "Paid" plan at signup (rather than the new "Free" plan, which self-closes after 6 months) is the correct way to guarantee uninterrupted indefinite Always-Free-tier access without ever needing to "reopen"/re-verify the account — this seems to be the practical answer to avoid the new 6-month Free-plan expiry gotcha, but wasn't explicitly spelled out as guidance on any page fetched.
- On GCP, check whether a Free Trial billing account (not yet manually upgraded to paid) continues to serve Cloud Functions/Cloud Scheduler Always-Free-tier usage after the 90-day trial auto-closes, or whether the billing account must be upgraded to "paid" status for Always Free products to keep functioning at all (the docs say the account "auto-closes" after 90 days/credit exhaustion — unclear if Always Free access survives that closure without upgrading).
- Cloud Build / Artifact Registry storage charges tied to Cloud Functions deployment (noted on cloud.google.com/run/pricing) could be a small non-zero, easy-to-overlook cost source for GCP even at trivial invocation volumes — worth a follow-up numeric check.
- If Frost Alert needs several distinct hour-offset checks that can't be expressed as one cron's comma-list (e.g., different logic/target per offset), GCP's per-job billing model ($0.10/job beyond 3 free/account) vs. AWS's per-invocation model (14M free) may tip the cost comparison toward AWS at accounts with multiple job/schedule definitions across other GCP use — worth quantifying against the user's actual schedule design.
