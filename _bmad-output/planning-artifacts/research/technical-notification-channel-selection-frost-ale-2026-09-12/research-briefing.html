Executive summary

Pick Telegram Bot API, with Pushover as the strongest runner-up. Both natively support two-way acknowledgment (Telegram's inline-keyboard callback_query; Pushover's Emergency-Priority callback on ack), are genuinely free or near-free at hobby volume, need no custom app, and show no Sweden/EU blocker.

ntfy.sh is a close, fully free third option with a documented ack mechanism but a real (mitigable) Android delivery-timing caveat on its default build. WhatsApp Business/Cloud API is technically workable but sits in direct, current tension with its own Terms of Service ("not for personal, family, or household purposes") — a risk none of the other four candidates carry — plus real onboarding friction and a non-zero per-message cost from October 2026. Email + action link is a viable, cheap fallback, but its ack mechanism is the least purpose-built of the five.

Drivers: (1) only Telegram, Pushover, and ntfy.sh have an ack mechanism designed for exactly this pattern; (2) WhatsApp's ToS conflict is primary-sourced and independently corroborated, not inferred; (3) all five candidates clear the Sweden/EU bar — this dimension didn't differentiate the field as expected.

Biggest caveat: no candidate's Android push-delivery reliability was independently benchmarked end-to-end. Every reliability claim rests on official docs, vendor troubleshooting copy, or community reports — mitigated the same way across all app-based candidates: explicitly whitelist the app from battery optimization.

Requirements frame

Hard gates: (1) no custom native app to build/maintain; (2) two-way acknowledgment (reply, button, or clickable link); (3) reliable push-style delivery to a stock Android phone.

Weighted preferences: cost (highest, $0 ideal) · delivery reliability (high) · setup complexity (medium) · Sweden/EU quirks (medium).

Telegram Bot API Pick

Mechanism

Inline-keyboard buttons are the purpose-built ack path: a tap sends a callback_query update, and the bot must call answerCallbackQuery to clear the client's loading spinner [1][2]. Two mutually exclusive retrieval modes: getUpdates (polling) or setWebhook (push) [1]. Because allowed_updates accepts callback_query, a cron job calling getUpdates can plausibly receive acks without an always-on webhook receiver medium confidence — inference, not a worked example [1]. A real webhook needs your own domain + HTTPS server [3].

Cost

Free at hobby scale; limits (~1 msg/sec/chat, ~30 msg/sec bulk) independently corroborated by the python-telegram-bot library [4][6] high.

Reliability

All delay evidence traces to generic Android battery-optimization/Doze behavior, not a Telegram-specific defect [5][7][8][9] medium. No quantitative latency benchmark exists.

Sweden/EU

EEA data in Netherlands data centers, EDPO as Art. 27 representative [11] high. Bot-operator's own GDPR obligations plausibly sidestepped by the household exemption, not independently confirmed [12] medium.

Ecosystem

Monthly-cadence releases through August 2026, no deprecation language found [1] high.

Pushover Runner-up

Mechanism

"Emergency Priority" (priority=2) messages repeat until acknowledged or expiry; an optional callback URL has Pushover's own servers POST to your endpoint the instant the user acknowledges [15][16] high — the most server-mediated ack of the five.

Cost

One-time $4.99/platform license, independently corroborated [13][14]; free to 10,000 msgs/month/account (per-account since 2026-05-01) [13] high.

Reliability

Sub-second typical delivery per official docs; recent delay reports trace to client-side IPv6 misconfiguration, a known issue since 2021, not a Pushover defect [17][18][19][20] medium-high.

Sweden/EU

Privacy policy (updated 2026-05-01) explicitly covers EEA legal bases, sub-processors, SCCs, data-subject rights [21] high.

Ecosystem

Solo-developer venture since 2012, actively shipping in 2026 [22][23][24] — single-point-of-failure risk, offset by 14 years of continuous operation medium.

ntfy.sh

Mechanism

An http action button fires a client-side request to an arbitrary URL on tap [25][26] high; one known parsing quirk has a documented workaround [27].

Cost

Public server free indefinitely for personal use [28]. Live-verified free-tier limits (250 msgs/day) [41] verified — comfortably covers hobby volume. Open-source self-hosting fallback available [32][33].

Reliability

Real trade-off: default Play-Store path uses Firebase Cloud Messaging, which the maintainer himself calls "not great" for timeliness; the low-latency "instant delivery" mode can itself be killed by Doze/battery optimization [34][35][36] medium. Active fixes shipped Jul-Aug 2026 [40].

Sweden/EU

US/Connecticut-governed, no stated EU data residency [37][38] mild flag, non-blocking — mitigated by self-hosting.

Ecosystem

~33.6k GitHub stars, steady release cadence, single lead maintainer plus contributors [42][43][44] high.

Mechanism

Interactive reply buttons fire a webhook with the button ID/title [45] high, but any cold-start message must use a pre-approved template — heavier setup than the others [50].

Cost

~$0.018–0.020/delivered message for Sweden's "Rest of Western Europe" tier [55][56] medium — a few dollars/year, not $0; Meta's own roadmap ends the current fee exemption 2026-10-01 [50][51] high.

The decisive issue

WhatsApp's Business ToS explicitly prohibits use "for personal, family, or household purposes" [57] — independently verified against a second primary source [61] verified. Pre-existing clause (since Feb 2024), enforcement against a hobby bot is discretionary/undocumented [58], but it's a live tension none of the other candidates carry.

Setup friction

Free test number capped at 5 recipients, widely reported to expire ~every 90 days [48][49] medium.

Reliability

Only unsourced, promotional vendor-blog figures found [52][53] low.

Email + action link

Mechanism

GET renders an inert confirmation, a separate POST flips the state — never consume on the initial GET [62] high. Outlook Safe Links prefetching is a recurring, well-documented risk across auth libraries [63][64][65]; no comparable Gmail-side issue was found, but it also wasn't ruled out open question.

Cost

Resend, Mailgun, and Brevo all hold genuine, non-time-limited $0 tiers [66][67][71] high. Gmail SMTP (500/rolling-24h) works as a zero-setup fallback [70]. Avoid SendGrid (60-day trial only from 2025-03-25 [68][69]) and note SES's free tier is now a generic AWS-credit model [71].

Deliverability & reliability

Gmail's strict bulk-sender rules only bite above ~5,000/day [75]; a single engaged recipient eliminates complaint-rate risk [78]. Android sync can take up to 15 minutes [79], mitigated by setting battery usage to unrestricted [80][81].

Sweden/EU

GDPR household exemption very likely applies [82]; Sweden's IMY guidance targets organizations, not individuals [85] medium.

Cross-dimension insights

  • The Sweden/EU dimension didn't differentiate the field as expected — all five clear it with at most mild flags. The real differentiator was mechanism purpose-fit: Telegram/Pushover/ntfy have ack features designed for this pattern; WhatsApp/email repurpose general features.
  • The two cheapest options on paper (ntfy, email) share the same underlying Android background-sync/Doze dependency as the "native-feeling" push services. There's no free lunch on reliability — the highest-leverage mitigation across the board is disabling battery optimization for the chosen app.
  • WhatsApp is the only candidate where cost and compliance point the same direction — the only real recurring bill and the only live ToS conflict. Both independently argue against it as primary.

Recommendations

  1. Primary: Telegram Bot API. Free, purpose-built ack, no verification friction, favorable EU data residency. Validate the cron-polled getUpdates-without-webhook hosting shape with a short proof-of-concept before committing (medium-confidence inference).
  2. Runner-up: Pushover. Fall back to this if the Telegram PoC fails, or if the single-maintainer risk profile is a concern either way. Its server-mediated callback-on-ack is arguably an even cleaner architectural fit.
  3. Do not select WhatsApp as primary. Verified ToS conflict + template friction + ~90-day test-number churn + real (small) per-message cost make it the weakest fit despite being technically capable. Treat future use only as an informed risk acceptance.
  4. Keep ntfy.sh and email+action-link as secondary/fallback channels — defense in depth against any single channel's delivery-timing risk, rather than the sole channel.

Open questions

  • Does a cron-triggered getUpdates call actually receive callback_query updates in practice? Resolve with a 10-minute proof-of-concept.
  • Does Gmail perform any link-prefetch/scanning comparable to Outlook's Safe Links? Test against the actual target Gmail account.
  • What is Meta's first-party (not BSP-republished) rate for "Rest of Western Europe"? Low priority given WhatsApp isn't the pick.
  • No candidate's real-world Android push latency was independently benchmarked under Doze/battery-optimization conditions — a short empirical test on the actual target phone would close this gap directly.
