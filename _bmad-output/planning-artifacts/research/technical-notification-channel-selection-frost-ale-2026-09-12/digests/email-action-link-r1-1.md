# Digest: Email + action link (round 1)

## Findings

1. **[mechanism]** Standard secure magic-link pattern: GET renders an inert confirmation page; an explicit user-triggered POST (button click / auto-submitted JS form) actually consumes the token/flips state — never consume on the initial GET. — Medium (Obie Fernandez), referenced by 2024-2026 GitHub threads, confidence: high.
2. **[mechanism]** Outlook/Office 365 "Safe Links" rewrites and prefetches URLs (HEAD/GET) before the user clicks, which can consume single-use tokens early and invalidate links — a documented, recurring issue across next-auth, FusionAuth, Supabase auth trackers. — GitHub issue threads (2020-2024, still referenced in 2026), confidence: high.
3. **[mechanism]** Mitigations: no-op HEAD requests in validation logic, require explicit POST to change state, or fall back to OTP/code-entry when scanners are a concern. Primarily an Outlook/Office 365/corporate-scanner issue; Gmail's own link handling was not reported as a comparable prefetch-invalidation problem in these threads. — GitHub (FusionAuth), confidence: medium.
4. **[pricing]** Resend free tier: 3,000 emails/month, hard cap 100/day, 1 verified domain, 30-day retention, 10,000 automation runs — genuine permanent free tier. — Resend official pricing, confidence: high.
5. **[pricing]** Mailgun free plan: 100 emails/day, 1 custom domain, 1 API-key pair (limit 2), 1-day log retention, 1 inbound route — permanent free but shared IPs, with reports of weaker deliverability on free-tier shared IPs to Yahoo/Hotmail/AOL. — Mailgun official pricing + help center, confidence: high.
6. **[pricing]** SendGrid killed its permanent free tier as of ~March/May 2025: new direct accounts get only a 60-day trial capped at 100/day, then must pay $19.95/mo+; sendgrid.com now redirects to Twilio.com. — Twilio/SendGrid official support article, confidence: high.
7. **[pricing]** Amazon SES has no SES-specific free tier for new customers as of mid-2025/2026 (new-customer model moved to general AWS Free Tier credits, ~$200 over 6 months); standard pricing thereafter $0.16/1,000 emails (Essentials tier, 0-10M/mo). — AWS official blog + pricing page, confidence: high.
8. **[pricing]** Brevo free plan: 300 emails/day (shared transactional+marketing), no credit card required, permanent; overflow beyond 300/day queues up to 1,000 for retry, excess dropped, unused quota doesn't roll over. — Brevo official help center, confidence: high.
9. **[pricing]** Personal Gmail via SMTP + app password can send up to 500 messages/recipients per rolling 24h window ("less secure apps" fully removed May 2025) — far more than a single-recipient alert needs. — Nylas dev docs / Smartlead help center (both citing Google's published limits), confidence: medium.
10. **[ecosystem]** SendGrid's permanent free plan retired industry-wide (sources disagree slightly on exact date, March vs. May 2025, but agree it's gone for new direct signups). — Dreamlit blog (secondary) + Twilio/SendGrid (primary), confidence: medium.
11. **[ecosystem]** SES's free tier was already cut once before (Aug 2023, 62,000/mo for EC2 senders down to 3,000/mo) and restructured again in 2025 to a generic AWS-credit model — a pattern of shrinking/changing free tiers in this space. — Emercury blog (secondary, cites AWS history), confidence: medium.
12. **[ecosystem]** Resend, Mailgun, and Brevo currently retain permanent free tiers as of this check — the more stable free options relative to SendGrid/SES, which have both cut/restructured recently. — official pricing/help pages (3 sources), confidence: high.
13. **[deliverability]** Gmail recommends SPF/DKIM/DMARC (min p=none) for all senders; stricter bulk-sender enforcement only kicks in above ~5,000 msgs/day to personal Gmail — a single-recipient hobby sender is far under this threshold. — Google official admin/help docs, confidence: high.
14. **[deliverability]** Passing SPF/DKIM/DMARC doesn't guarantee inbox placement — Gmail also weighs reputation, engagement, content, and volume consistency; low-volume/newly-active senders can be treated as riskier, and volume spikes after quiet periods can trigger temporary rate-limiting/spam-foldering. — Relaymetry, Suped (secondary deliverability blogs, consistent with Google's own guidance), confidence: medium.
15. **[deliverability]** Google recommends spam-complaint rate <0.1% (never reach 0.3%); for a single recipient who never marks mail as spam, this risk factor is effectively eliminated regardless of provider. — Google official, confidence: high.
16. **[reliability]** Gmail Android app relies on background "Sync Gmail"; can be delayed by battery optimization/Adaptive Battery/Data Saver/OEM restrictions; Google's own docs say sync can take up to 15 minutes, longer after device idle. — Google official Gmail Help, confidence: high.
17. **[reliability]** Mitigation: set Gmail's battery usage to "Unrestricted"/"Don't optimize," ensure background data + Sync Gmail enabled, disable Battery Saver/Data Saver — standard fixes across multiple troubleshooting guides. — iTechHacks, Technobezz (secondary), confidence: medium.
18. **[compliance]** GDPR Art. 2(2)(c) exempts processing by a natural person "in the course of a purely personal or household activity" (Recital 18 cites personal correspondence). A hobbyist emailing themselves alerts via a US-based provider, as a private non-commercial single-user system, should fall within this exemption — GDPR controller obligations likely don't apply. — DPO World, LegalClarity (citing Art. 2(2)(c), Recital 18, CJEU Ryneš C-212/13), confidence: medium.
19. **[compliance]** The exemption has limits per case law (Ryneš) if processing extends beyond a purely private sphere (e.g. public-facing systems, third-party data) — not directly applicable here since Frost Alert processes only the hobbyist's own data, but worth flagging if ever extended to other users. — LegalClarity, confidence: low.
20. **[compliance]** No Sweden-specific restriction found on an individual using US-based email/SMTP providers personally; Sweden's IMY (data protection authority) guidance on email security targets organizations/data controllers, not private individuals' personal use. — IMY official, confidence: medium.

## Not found

## Verdict
Credible, low-cost, low-maintenance fit for this single-user, single-recipient use case. High confidence on mechanics and pricing: Resend, Mailgun, and Brevo all currently offer genuine $0-forever tiers (100-300 emails/day) that vastly exceed Frost Alert's handful of alerts/month, and Gmail SMTP+app-password (500/rolling-24h) works as a zero-setup fallback — but avoid SendGrid (free tier killed 2025) and note SES's free tier is now a generic AWS-credit model rather than an ongoing allowance. The GET-renders/POST-flips magic-link pattern is a well-documented, proven mitigation against link-prefetching (high confidence, though best-documented for Outlook, not confirmed for Gmail specifically — flagged as a lead, residual risk plausibly low). Deliverability to a single engaged Gmail recipient who never marks mail as spam should be favorable with basic SPF/DKIM (medium-high confidence). Main soft risk is Android notification promptness — dependent on Gmail's background sync, mitigated by setting battery usage to unrestricted (high confidence, from Google's own docs). On Sweden/EU: the personal/household GDPR exemption very likely applies and no Sweden-specific blocker was found, though this is legal interpretation rather than a regulator ruling on this exact scenario (medium confidence). Recommend Resend or Mailgun (Gmail SMTP as zero-setup fallback) over SendGrid/SES given their recent free-tier instability.
