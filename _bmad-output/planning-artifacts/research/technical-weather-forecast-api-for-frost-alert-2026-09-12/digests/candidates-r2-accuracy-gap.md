# Round 2: Closing the Sweden/Nordic overnight-low accuracy benchmark gap

**Mission:** Find (or definitively rule out) a Sweden/Nordic-specific, overnight-low-temperature-specific, independent accuracy benchmark for SMHI, Open-Meteo (and underlying models), or MET Norway/Yr.

**Bottom line up front:** The gap is **not closeable** with a clean head-to-head number. No source found in this session gives a Sweden-specific, minimum-temperature-specific skill score comparing SMHI vs. MET Norway vs. any Open-Meteo-hosted model at 24h/48h lead time. What *does* exist: (a) both SMHI and MET Norway run continuous operational verification programs and publish methodology/summary pages, (b) MET Norway's own verification report contains a **documented case study of minimum-temperature forecast failure** in Nordic radiative-cooling conditions (single case, not a skill score), (c) SMHI stopped issuing agricultural frost warnings in 2009, (d) well-established meteorological science confirms radiative-cooling nights are structurally harder to forecast than advective nights, which explains *why* a clean benchmark is unlikely to exist in the first place.

## Claims

- SMHI continuously verifies temperature (and wind, precipitation) forecasts against ~180 stations across Sweden, comparing the 08:00 and 20:00 forecast issued ~0, 3, and 5 days ahead against observations taken every 3 hours around the clock; a temperature forecast is counted as "correct" only if it is within ±2°C of the observation, and results are published as a monthly, per-county "percent correct" — this is a general temperature verification (not broken out by minimum-temperature or overnight-specific skill, and not published as a lead-time-specific skill score beyond the "issued ~0/3/5 days ago" comparison) — source: SMHI, "Uppföljning av prognoser," https://www.smhi.se/data/temperatur-och-vind/temperatur/uppfoljning-av-prognoser, published: 2025-02-05 (updated 2025-09-23), accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: high, class: accuracy
- SMHI's own explainer on forecast-skill measurement confirms there is no common cross-institute standard for measuring forecast quality, that SMHI's current verification method dates to 2017, and that it explicitly only reports aggregate ± 2 °C "hit rate" for temperature — it does not describe any published minimum-temperature-specific or overnight-specific breakdown — source: SMHI, "Hur mäts prognosers träffsäkerhet?," https://www.smhi.se/kunskapsbanken/meteorologi/vaderprognoser/hur-mats-prognosers-traffsakerhet, published: undated, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: high, class: accuracy
- SMHI has discontinued issuing official frost warnings for agriculture since the 2009 season; it now only mentions frost risk within general public forecasts, meaning there is no current SMHI product (or associated verification report) specifically targeting agricultural minimum-temperature/frost forecasting in Sweden — source: SMHI, "Frost och markfrost," https://www.smhi.se/kunskapsbanken/meteorologi/sno--och-isfenomen/frost-och-markfrost, published: undated, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: high, class: accuracy
- SMHI does produce statistical post-processing (adaptive Kalman filtering, "Perfect Prog," and Model-Output-Statistics-style regression corrections) specifically to reduce systematic temperature-forecast error, including a worked example correcting ECMWF global-model 12h temperature forecasts for Sodankylä (northern Finland) — this shows SMHI's methodology for improving temperature accuracy generally, but is not itself a minimum-temperature accuracy/skill-score report — source: SMHI, "Statistiska korrektioner av fel i numeriska prognoser" and "Kalmanfiltrering av numeriska prognoser," https://www.smhi.se/kunskapsbanken/meteorologi/meteorologiska-modeller/statistiska-korrektioner-av-fel-i-numeriska-prognoser, published: undated, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: medium, class: accuracy
- MET Norway publishes an annual/periodic "METinfo" verification report series (e.g., MET-info 24-2022, MET-info 31-2024, MET-info 27-2026) that verifies the main operational NWP models it runs, including **MEPS** — the MetCoOp ensemble covering **Norway, Sweden, Finland, and the Baltic states** — using standard continuous-variable metrics (mean error, MAE, RMSE, standard deviation of error, correlation) and categorical skill scores (Hanssen-Kuipers, Heidke, ETS) for thresholded variables — source: Norwegian Meteorological Institute, "METinfo verification report," MET-info 31-2024, https://www.met.no/publikasjoner/met-info/met-info-2024/_/attachment/inline/00476eb6-c021-4601-beb5-87e29c8f96f9:8de05906dd220c52fa401e7c2f16491b569a6907/MET-info-31-2024.pdf, published: 2024, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: high, class: accuracy
- MET Norway's MET-info 31-2024 verification report documents, as a named recurring problem ("has persisted through the last years"), a **positive bias in MEPS 2m-temperature forecasts during very cold nighttime-minimum/cold-inversion events**; the report's worked case (15–16 January event, Dagali, southeastern Norway) shows the MEPS control run forecasting a minimum of roughly −20°C when the observed low reached −34°C, and attributes the failure to the model being unable to reproduce the rapid near-surface temperature drop despite correctly forecast cloudiness — i.e., a surface/lowest-model-layer exchange problem, not a radiation-scheme error. This is a single documented case study from one primary source, not an aggregated skill score, but it is a rare piece of concrete, Nordic-domain evidence of minimum-temperature forecast failure mode — source: Norwegian Meteorological Institute, "METinfo verification report," MET-info 31-2024, https://www.met.no/publikasjoner/met-info/met-info-2024/_/attachment/inline/00476eb6-c021-4601-beb5-87e29c8f96f9:8de05906dd220c52fa401e7c2f16491b569a6907/MET-info-31-2024.pdf, published: 2024, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: medium, class: accuracy
- MET Norway operates a public verification dashboard (verification.met.no) offering "station verification" (including extreme-weather events) and "summary verification," but the page itself only exposes navigation links to interactive tools — no static aggregate minimum-temperature skill-score figures were retrievable via a direct page fetch in this session; using it would require interactive/JS-driven exploration beyond this session's tooling — source: Norwegian Meteorological Institute, "Verification," https://verification.met.no/, published: undated, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: medium, class: accuracy
- DWD's own 2019 verification comparison of its ICON global model against ECMWF's IFS (for the old COSMO-EU European domain, i.e., not Sweden-specific) found that at shorter lead times (12h) ICON's quality is comparable to IFS, with ICON showing an advantage for 2m temperature and relative humidity in winter specifically, while at longer lead times IFS becomes increasingly better than ICON — this is the closest primary-source, model-vs-model temperature comparison found for models that Open-Meteo exposes, but it is European-domain (not Sweden/Nordic-specific), not minimum-temperature-specific (it is general T2M), and is now roughly 7 years old, well outside the ≤2-year freshness bar for verification stats — source: ECMWF/DWD, "Application and verification of ECMWF products – Germany," https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2019/19197-application-and-verification-ecmwf-products-2019-germany.pdf, published: 2019, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: low, class: accuracy
- An NWP model intercomparison for the European Arctic (Year of Polar Prediction Special Observing Period) found that higher-resolution regional models generally forecast lower (colder) minimum temperatures than the coarser global ECMWF IFS-HRES, and specifically noted that the Météo-France AROME setup shows a pronounced cold/negative bias in the mountainous terrain on the Norway–Sweden border — this is Nordic-terrain-relevant and touches minimum temperature, but the study domain is the Arctic/high-latitude polar-prediction period, not Sweden's typical frost-relevant lowland/coastal terrain, and it is a research intercomparison rather than an operational verification product — source: "An NWP Model Intercomparison of Surface Weather Parameters in the European Arctic during YOPP SOP-NH1," https://applicate-h2020.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/an-nwp-model-intercomparison-of-surface-weather-parameters-in-the-european-arctic-during-the-year-of-polar-prediction-special-observing-period-northern-hemisphere.pdf, published: ~2020, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: low, class: accuracy
- EUMETNET's SRNWP-V programme (Short-Range NWP Verification) built a common verification framework comparing HIRLAM, ALADIN, COSMO, the UK Unified Model, and ECMWF's global model on 2m temperature (among other parameters) against station observations across Europe, but the retrieved materials show only bias/RMSE-style comparisons without a clear, Sweden-specific or minimum-temperature-specific ranking, and none of the models in this specific comparison map cleanly onto SMHI/MET Norway/Open-Meteo's current production lineup — source: EUMETNET SRNWP-V, "SRNWP-V Programme: a Comparison of Regional European Forecast Models," https://docslib.org/doc/1140515/srnwp-v-programme-a-comparison-of-regional-european-forecast-models, published: ~2011, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: low, class: accuracy
- Academic literature on Nordic/Fennoscandian frost prediction that was found concerns **time-to-first-hard-freeze** at seasonal lead times (weeks to months), not the 24–48h overnight-low lead time relevant to a frost-alert automation; e.g., a postprocessed multimodel seasonal ensemble for Fennoscandia (covering Norway and parts of Sweden/Finland/Russia) beats a climatology baseline only where the climatological time-to-freeze is under ~40 days, with skill declining at longer lead times and weakest near coasts — useful context on Nordic frost-forecast skill in general, but not applicable to the short-lead-time, minimum-temperature-accuracy question this mission targets — source: Roksvåg et al., "Probabilistic prediction of the time to hard freeze using seasonal weather forecasts and survival time methods," Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society, https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.4403, published: 2022, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: medium, class: accuracy
- It is well-established, long-standing meteorological science (not new to this session, but corroborated by multiple independent practitioner/training sources) that radiatively-cooled (clear-sky, calm, dry) overnight minimum temperatures are structurally harder for NWP models to forecast accurately than advectively-driven minimum temperatures, because the radiative case depends on hyper-local factors that models struggle to resolve at typical grid resolution — cloud-cover errors, boundary-layer decoupling under light wind, dewpoint/humidity, snow cover, soil moisture, and terrain — any of which can shift the actual low by several degrees from model consensus; MET Norway's own 2024 case study (above) is a concrete, Nordic-specific illustration of exactly this failure mode (correct cloud forecast, but the model could not reproduce the rapid near-surface cooling) — source: National Weather Service (Zhu/JAX training), "Atmospheric Controllers of Local Nighttime Temperature," https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/winds/nighttime_influences/Nighttime_Influences.htm, published: undated (long-standing forecaster training material), accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: medium, class: accuracy; corroborating source: theweatherprediction.com, "Forecasting Trick Series: Strong Radiational Cooling," https://www.theweatherprediction.com/habyhints2/423/, published: undated, accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: low, class: accuracy
- A 2025/2026 preprint developed an automated machine-learning framework for next-day minimum-temperature forecasting across eight climatically diverse Western European sites, explicitly motivated by the fact that "numerical weather prediction models' performance for Tmin forecasting remains limited by difficulties in representing fine-scale nocturnal processes" — this independently corroborates the radiative-cooling difficulty claim from a recent academic source, and demonstrates ML post-processing can materially improve on raw NWP/climatology baselines (35–54% MAE improvement over persistence, 51–64% over climatology at the tested sites), though none of the eight sites are confirmed to be in Sweden or the Nordic region based on the retrieved abstract — source: "Spatial Predictor Selection for Next-Day Minimum Temperature Forecasting: An Automated Machine Learning Framework Applied Across European Climate Regimes," https://doi.org/10.31223/x55758, published: 2025/2026 (EarthArXiv preprint), accessed: 2026-09-12, confidence: low, class: accuracy

## Leads

- **verification.met.no** is an interactive dashboard (station-level and summary verification, including extreme-weather-event verification) that could not be mined for static figures with a plain page fetch in this session. A follow-up session with browser automation/JS rendering could potentially extract an actual minimum-temperature skill score for Nordic stations, possibly including Swedish MEPS-domain stations.
- MET-info reports are published periodically (2022, 2024, and apparently a 2026 edition was found in search results but not opened in depth in this session) — the newest MET-info 27-2026 PDF was found but not inspected for temperature-specific content; it may contain a more recent minimum-temperature case study or updated bias statistics.
- SMHI's commercial energy-sector product ("Väderprognoser för fjärrvärme och elförbrukning") explicitly mentions sending customers a "monthly verification report evaluating the forecast's performance" for temperature (including a coldest-scenario forecast). This report is not public, but SMHI customer service could plausibly be asked whether any aggregate/anonymized version exists — this is a private/commercial artifact, not something retrievable via web search.
- Open-Meteo's own Historical Forecast API and Previous Runs API are explicitly designed to let a user reconstruct past model forecasts and compare them against later "ground truth" analyses — i.e., Open-Meteo provides the *tooling* to build a custom Sweden-specific, minimum-temperature-specific accuracy benchmark (comparing `ecmwf_ifs025`, `dwd_icon`/`dwd_icon_eu`, and MET Norway's Nordic model against station observations) even though no one has apparently published such a benchmark yet. This is a strong candidate for a DIY approach if the project wants a real answer rather than relying on secondary literature.
- The EUMETNET SRNWP-V intercomparison and the WMO Commission for Basic Systems (CBS) global-model verification exchange were both referenced as frameworks but their current/recent output (post-2020) was not retrieved in this session; CBS in particular publishes long-running global-model verification statistics (including T2M-adjacent parameters) that were not directly searched here and could be a next angle.
- The Arctic YOPP intercomparison paper's finding that "high-resolution models forecast the lowest minimum temperatures" (i.e., a systematic difference in minimum-temperature bias between high-res regional models like AROME/MEPS and the coarser global IFS-HRES) is directly relevant to the "which model family is better for Tmin" question, but this session did not extract quantitative bias/RMSE figures split specifically by minimum temperature at Swedish stations from that paper — worth a deeper read of the full text file saved this session.

## Could not find

- **No Sweden-specific minimum/overnight-temperature skill score for SMHI, MET Norway/Yr, or any Open-Meteo-hosted model was found**, despite English- and Swedish-language queries covering practitioner, academic, and institutional angles ("SMHI prognosverifiering," "minimitemperatur prognos träffsäkerhet," "MET Norway forecast verification skill score minimum temperature," EUMETNET/WMO intercomparisons, agromet frost-warning studies). This confirms the prior research round's finding: this specific gap appears to be genuinely unaddressed in public, citable sources as of this session.
- **No head-to-head comparison of SMHI vs. MET Norway vs. Open-Meteo (or their underlying models) at 24h/48h lead time for minimum temperature, for Sweden or comparable Nordic terrain, exists in any source retrieved this session.** SMHI's public verification page reports only a general (not minimum-temperature-specific) ±2°C "hit rate" aggregated by county and month; it does not publish model-vs-model comparisons at all (SMHI's own products are statistically post-processed blends, not raw single-model output the public verification page attributes to a named model).
- **No WMO or EUMETNET report was found that isolates minimum-temperature skill specifically for Sweden or a comparable Nordic/coastal-continental climate.** The EUMETNET SRNWP-V materials retrieved cover general 2m temperature bias/RMSE across broader European domains and involve legacy models (HIRLAM, ALADIN, COSMO, UM) that do not map cleanly onto the three candidates' current production systems.
- **No agricultural-extension or agromet frost-warning-system study specific to Sweden was found that benchmarks forecast-provider accuracy.** Sweden's official agricultural pest/disease forecasting service (Jordbruksverket's "Prognos och Varning") uses local weather-station networks and temperature-sum models for pest/disease timing, not minimum-temperature/frost forecast verification, and SMHI has not issued official agricultural frost warnings since 2009. Commercial hyperlocal-forecast vendors (e.g., Cordulus) make accuracy claims (e.g., "50% more accurate than others") in marketing copy only, with no methodology or independent verification found — this is exactly the kind of unsupported marketing claim the brief asked to flag rather than treat as evidence, so it is deliberately excluded from the Claims section above.
- **No source quantifies MET Norway's MEPS minimum-temperature cold-bias problem as an aggregate statistic** (e.g., "X°C average warm bias on clear calm nights below −10°C across Y stations"). The only concrete evidence found is the single named case study (Dagali, 15–16 January) in the MET-info 31-2024 report, explicitly framed by MET Norway itself as illustrative of a recurring problem, not as a systematic skill score.
- Given all of the above, **the two-source-class bar for any specific accuracy/skill-score number or definitive comparative provider claim cannot be met.** Every concrete number found (the MEPS Dagali case, the DWD-vs-ECMWF ICON/IFS comparison, the ML preprint's MAE improvement figures) comes from a single primary source, is domain-mismatched to Sweden specifically, or both. No claim in this digest should be read as "Provider A is more accurate than Provider B at overnight lows in Sweden" — that comparative claim remains unsupported by any evidence retrieved in either research round.
